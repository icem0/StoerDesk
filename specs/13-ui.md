# 14 — UI

**Chapter:** 14
**Scope:** ~140 lines
**Status:** reviewed for phase 16 (WP-M16-00)

The binding summary is `PLAN.md` §14. This slice details the shell of the
guarded interface, its header menu, the colours of the instance and the shell
on a phone. `PLAN.md` §14 is copied here and reviewed; the new sections name
what phase 16 adds.

## 14.1 Surfaces

Public:

- report form `/m/:token`,
- status page `/s/:token`.

Internal:

- dashboard,
- ticket list with filters, search and saved views ("mine", "pool", "overdue"),
- ticket detail with history, photos, timeline and role/status actions,
- on-call calendar,
- object list,
- statistics,
- settings (users, groups, standard texts, deadlines, on-call grid, channels,
  API keys).

## 14.2 Requirements

- mobile-first, one-hand usable report form,
- desktop two-column layout (list left, detail right),
- fixed action bar at the bottom on mobile ticket detail,
- photo gallery with fullscreen and download,
- accessibility: AA contrast, touch targets at least 44 px, status not conveyed
  by colour alone,
- dark mode via `prefers-color-scheme`, plus a theme per user (`auto`, `light`,
  `dark`) that the user management sets (WP-M2-92),
- all text through i18n.

## 14.3 The shell

One shell (`apps/web/src/components/Shell.tsx`) frames every guarded page
(WP-M1-13, WP-M1-17). It carries the logo and the company name of the branding,
the app version, the header menu and the theme of the user. The content of a
page goes into one `main` inside it.

The header menu (WP-M2-28, WP-M10-35) holds the entries the internal user needs
without opening the settings page — the ticket list, the statistics and the
readiness. On a phone the menu wraps onto its own row and scrolls horizontally
as a tab strip of 44 px touch targets.

The settings gear (WP-M1-17) links to the settings overview `SettingsPage`
(WP-M12-07), which groups every settings and administration surface by topic.
There is no dropdown.

## 14.4 The accent colour of the instance (ADR-0036)

The interface has **one accent colour** that paints what asks for attention:
the active entry of the header menu, the links of the settings menu, the focus
ring and the badge of unread messages. It is not a build decision and not a
user preference — it belongs to the instance and the administration configures
it once, under „Darstellung“ next to the company name, the logo and the colour
of the internal comment.

- The colour travels in the `branding` settings row as `accentColor`, a hex
  value `#rrggbb`, default `#0ea5e9` — the blue the interface painted before.
- `GET /api/v1/settings/branding` answers it for the shell; `GET
  /api/v1/settings/display` answers it beside the colour of the internal
  comment, because a colour of the presentation is no secret and the ticket
  view needs both.
- One save of the Darstellung writes the whole row: company name, title switch,
  logo, internal comment colour and accent colour. A body without the accent
  keeps the stored one.
- A value that is no hex colour never reaches the server and never leaves the
  browser; the page names the mistake and sends nothing.
- Since WP-M16-06 each colour under „Darstellung“ also has a **colour picker**:
  the browser picker of `input type="color"` is prefilled with the stored colour
  and writes the picked tone into the same state as the hex field beside it, so
  whichever of the two was changed last is the colour that is saved. The picker
  only answers `#rrggbb`, so the rule of the hex value above still holds.
- The **primary action of a page** wears the accent as well: the buttons that
  save, create or start something are painted with it, while the semantic colours
  of a state, of a channel or of a readiness keep their meaning.
- The shell writes the colour into the CSS custom property `--brand-accent` on
  its root. `styles.css` derives the ramp `--color-accent-300 … --color-accent-700`
  from it and every interface class paints `*-accent-*` instead of a fixed
  Tailwind step. The light theme mixes the same colour towards black.

## 14.5 The inbox inside the menu

The unread messages of the internal notification layer (WP-M14-120) are
reachable from the shell. The bell is no second action cluster in the header —
it is an **entry of the header menu** right after the readiness entry, so the
menu holds everything the internal user opens every day: tickets, statistics,
readiness, inbox.

The entry keeps the badge of unread messages of WP-M14-120e: the count above
the icon, `99+` above ninety-nine, no badge when every message is read, and one
click that opens `/notifications`. Without a session the entry is not rendered.

## 14.6 The shell on a phone

- The header stays in two rows: logo and title, then the menu as a tab strip
  that scrolls horizontally only — no vertical drift inside the strip.
- Every tap target of the strip is at least 44 px high and the strip reaches
  the edge of the screen, so a thumb does not aim between the tabs.
- The safe areas of a notch stay respected at the left, the right and the
  bottom edge of the shell.
- Tables scroll with momentum instead of clipping their columns (WP-M1-17).

## 14.7 The entries of the house in the ticket history (ADR-0034, ADR-0038)

The history of a ticket mixes two kinds of rows: the steps of the ticket —
review, work order, assign or offer, accept, acknowledge, close — and the
chatter of the house, which the customer never sees: the internal comment, the
firing of an escalation rule, an escalation note, the manual stage of a historic
ticket and the delivery of a message with its channel, its addressee and its
target.

- The rows of the house are **framed**: a 2 px border of `rounded-lg` in the
  colour of the internal entries under „Darstellung“ (the field
  `internalCommentColor` of ADR-0034, default dark red) beside a faint
  backdrop. The office sees at a glance which rows never left to the customer.
- The workflow steps and the steps of the pause stay unframed: they are the
  story of the ticket.
- The frame is a shape, not a colour alone: the row still names itself with its
  localized event type (§14.2).
- One list decides both sides (ADR-0038): `INTERNAL_TICKET_EVENT_TYPES` in
  `packages/shared/src/schemas/ticket.ts` names the internal event types, the
  ticket view frames them and the prepared send view leaves them out of the
  printout and out of the copied e-mail. A technician, a channel and a target
  never travel in a document of the customer.

## 14.8 The localized name and the row menu (ADR-0043)

Every list of the administration that names an entry shows **one name**: the
name of the language the interface is set to. The machine key, the German name
and the English name do not stand next to each other in a row any more — a
language that is added later would add a column and break the layout of the
table.

- The name of a row is the text of the current locale (`i18n.language`); the
  fallback is `de` and then the machine key, so a row is never nameless.
- The **actions of the row sit behind one `…` button**. The button opens a
  menu with the actions of that row (edit, archive, delete …); the menu closes
  on a click beside it, on `Escape` and after an action was chosen. The list
  itself carries no action button any more.
- The **edit shows the key and every translation**: the machine key is readable
  — read-only where the entry is referenced by the workflow, the escalation or
  the tickets, editable where the API allows a rename — and the texts are
  editable, one field per language the entry carries.
- The lists that follow the rule: the fault types, the detected errors, the
  ticket statuses, the stages of the escalation, the groups, the roles, the
  users, the webhooks, the API keys and the readiness pools. The channels of
  `/admin/channels` are configuration cards — every card carries its own form —
  so they keep their form and name their channel once.
- The shared parts are `apps/web/src/components/row-menu.tsx` (the `…` button
  and its menu) and `apps/web/src/lib/localized-name.ts` (the name of the
  current locale and the record of the languages of an entry). Every list uses
  them, so the menu behaves the same everywhere.

The API answers the texts as it does today; the web composes the record of the
languages out of the answer. A language that arrives later is a field of the
edit dialog — the table does not know it.

## 14.9 The order of the list and the direction of the history

The list at `/tickets` has **one order**, and it is the order of the office
(WP-M16-24): the **open tickets first**, then the **urgency descending**
(`critical`, `high`, `normal`, `low`), then the **nearest deadline**, then the
ticket number as the tie-breaker. The closed and the cancelled tickets keep the
last places. The page asks the API for that order with `order=priority`; the
`direction` parameter belongs to the two orders by time and does not change this
order.

The switch under „Darstellung“ (WP-M16-24c) steers the **history of a ticket**,
not the list: `historySortDirection` is `'desc'` — the newest event of the
history on top — by default and `'asc'` — the oldest event on top — otherwise.
The direction is an instance value in the jsonb row `branding` under the key
`historySortDirection`; a row of an older version keeps the key
`ticketSortDirection`, and the API reads that key as long as the new one is
missing.

The texts of the switch name the history and not the list:
`branding.field.historySortDirection` („Verlauf: Neuestes zuerst“ /
„History: Newest first“) and `branding.hintHistorySortDirection` (the hint says
that the switch applies to the events of the history in the ticket view and
names both of its states).

## 14.10 The menu of the settings (WP-M29-02 … WP-M29-03)

The settings overview (`/settings`) lists every settings and administration surface
in one place, grouped by topic — but single-column. Every entry wears a symbol and
a short description that names what the area does; a search field filters name and
description; the sections are collapsible, so the page stays short while nothing
disappears behind a second entry point.

The menu contains settings and administration only: the daily work surfaces
(tickets, statistics, readiness) live in the header menu, so they do not stand in
the settings menu a second time, and „Meine Bereitschaft" leaves the menu as well
because it opens the same `/oncall` as the header entry. The alarm tone button of
the shell (WP-M25-09) and the badge of the open delivery problems stay where they
are — above the groups.

The groups are:

- „Mein Bereich“ — Meine Einstellungen, Benachrichtigungen,
- „Darstellung“ — Darstellung & Logo, Dashboard & TV,
- „Objekte“ — Objekte, QR-Aufkleber,
- „Verwaltung“ — four links to topic pages: Benutzer & Rechte, Meldungsablauf,
  Bereitschaft, Kanäle & Versand,
- „Konto“ — Abmelden.

The entry texts and the descriptions live under `app.settings.*` in `de` and `en`;
every key exists in both languages (`npm run i18n:check` proves it). The old
namespace `auth.links.*` is not used by the menu any more.

## 14.11 The pages of the administration (WP-M29-04 … WP-M29-05)

The administration no longer presses its modules one under another on one long
page. Every topic has its own overview page:

- `/admin/access` — Benutzer, Rollen, Gruppen,
- `/admin/workflow` — Status, Störungsarten, Eskalation, Standardwerte,
  Festgestellte Fehler,
- `/admin/readiness` — Bereitschaftsplan, Meine Bereitschaft,
- `/admin/messaging` — Kanäle, Zustellungsprobleme, Mailbox des Betriebs,
  Webhooks, API-Schlüssel, BMAcloud-Abgleich.

Every overview page links its modules with symbol, label and short description,
rendered by the same component as the settings menu (WP-M29-02). The channels page
becomes an overview of its channels; every channel gets its own page
(`/admin/channels/:kind`, e. g. Matrix with homeserver and room), reachable by one
link. All settings and administration pages keep exactly one `<main>` landmark
inside the shell, one heading and a back link to the settings (WP-M29-06 … WP-M29-08).
